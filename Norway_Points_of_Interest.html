Akerselva River Walk

The 9.8 km trail is well-signed. Start from Central Station where the trail runs along the river — or take tram no. 11 to its final stop, Kjelsås. From there, walk toward the Technical Museum; just behind it you reach the river and Maridalsvannet lake. You'll pass through forested areas before reaching the newer Nydalen area, then continue downhill past historical buildings, fishing and swimming spots, and several waterfalls, all the way back to the city centre. The walk back is about 8 km.

Self-guided route
Ekeberg Sculpture Park

Tram 18 or 19 from Central Station runs directly to the Ekebergparken stop — about 12–15 minutes. From the tram stop, walk up the short stairway path to the park entrance; the main viewpoint terrace is a 5-minute uphill walk from the tram. Tram 19 runs every 8–10 minutes during the day.

Open in Maps
Island Hop Around Oslofjord

From Aker Brygge, take a ferry to the islands around Oslofjord with a regular public transport ticket or an Oslo Pass — Hovedøya, Lindøya, Nakholmen, Bleikøya, Gressholmen, and Langøyene. On a nice summer day, bring a swimsuit for a dip in the fjord.

7 · Nordnes Peninsula Walk

1.8 miles (3 km) one-way — about 45 minutes without stopping. (Route from a Google notification walking map, saved 7/28/2026.)

Knøsesmauet — a picturesque street, one of the personal favorites on this route.
Cort Piil-smauet — a historic street said to date back to the Middle Ages, with a nice square at the top of the hill.
Ytre Markeveien — a pretty, quiet residential street.
Nordnesparken — a park at the tip of the peninsula, a good final destination before heading back to town. Look for the totem pole donated as a gift by the city of Seattle.
Strandgaten — a cute street lined with boutiques and cafés where locals like to hang out, on the loop back to the heart of town.
Bryggen Harbour view — the final stop, with an iconic view along the illuminated Vågen.
