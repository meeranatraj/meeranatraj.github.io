Five days across the old town, the museum island, and two boat trips out onto the water — City Hall, the Vasa, Skansen, and a Viking-age day trip to Birka.

Getting around by water: Stockholm's inner-city waterways are integrated directly into the SL public transit network — no separate tickets needed, just tap a contactless card or use an SL pass.
  • Djurgården Ferry (Line 82): traditional passenger vessels connecting Slussen (Gamla Stan), Skeppsholmen, and Allmänna gränd (Djurgården). Runs every 10 minutes at peak times.
  • Pendelbåt (Line 80): connects Nybroplan (near the city center) with the northern docks of Djurgården.

Stockholm City Hall — Guided Tour

10-minute walk from Central Station. City Hall is only accessible on a guided tour — famous for its architecture and grand ceremonial halls, and the location of the annual Nobel Prize banquet. Tour is ~45 minutes.

Remember to book online one week in advance — tickets are released for tours one week ahead.

Walk over the central bridges past Strömkajen, and cross the gilded-crown bridge onto Skeppsholmen (20-minute walk). Walk the tranquil, tree-lined coastlines to view the historic tall ships.

The Parliament House (Riksdag)

20-minute walk from Central Station down into Gamla Stan. Arrive early at the Visitors' Entrance (Riksgatan 3) to join the Art or Public Tour — about 55 minutes. There are roughly 4,000 works of art in the Riksdag, including paintings, sculptures, drawings, and textiles seen on the Art Tour.

Free ~55 min · no pre-booking Open in Maps

Royal Palace — Changing of the Guard

A 5-minute walk to the courtyard of the King of Sweden's official residence to watch the ceremony.

Nobel Prize Museum

Located in the Stockholm Stock Exchange Building. Guided tour included. Guided tours run Monday–Sunday at 10:15, 11:15, 13:15, 15:15, 16:15, and 17:15, plus Friday evenings at 19:15 — limited slots per tour, sign up at the entrance (pre-booking isn't possible).

Try the Nobel Ice Cream — vanilla ice cream topped with blackcurrant sorbet, raspberry sauce, a chocolate medal, and spun sugar, based on the dessert served at the Nobel Banquet.

Djurgården Museums

Aug 22 — Vasa, Nordiska Museet, Skansen
Slussen Pier ──[Line 82 Ferry 10 min]──> Djurgården ──[Walk 10 min]──> Vasa ──[Walk 10 min]──> History Museum

Line 82 Ferry to Djurgården

Head to the Slussen SL Ferry Terminal in Gamla Stan. Board the historic Line 82 wooden ferry (Djurgårdsfärjan) for a scenic 10-minute cruise across the harbor to Djurgården island (Allmänna gränd).

Open in Maps

Vasa Museum

Walk along the forested shoreline (10-minute walk). The most visited museum in Scandinavia — home to the Vasa, the world's best-preserved 17th-century ship. Must book in advance. Arrive 20 minutes before the film starts, drop bags at the lockers, watch the film, then join a guided tour, then wander the exhibits at your own pace. Showings at 10 AM and 5 PM; open until 8 PM on Wednesdays.

240 SEK Book in advance Open in Maps

Nordiska Museet (Nordic Museum)

A 2-minute walk over. One of Stockholm's most distinctive museums, dedicated to everyday life and cultural change across the Nordic region from the 16th century to today. Eat a quick lunch at the museum café.

17 SEK Open in Maps

Skansen Open-Air Museum

Take Tram line 7 or walk back onto Djurgården island. The world's oldest open-air museum — wander historical Swedish village homesteads, watch traditional crafts, and see the Nordic wildlife zoo (bears, moose, wolves). Book tickets in advance.

Birka Viking Island

Klara Mälarstrand Pier

Walk from Central Station down to the boat pier (5-minute walk). Board the Strömma cruise liner M/S Mälar Victoria for a scenic 2-hour cruise westward across Lake Mälaren.

Open in Maps

Birka Island — 3.5 hours

A UNESCO World Heritage site on the island of Björkö, considered one of the most significant Viking Age archaeological sites in Scandinavia. Most visitors come as part of a guided tour from the city centre; local ferries are also an option if you'd rather skip the guided tour.

12:15 PM — Included 45-minute archaeological walking tour through the burial mounds.
1:00 PM — Viking-inspired vegetarian lunch at Restaurant Särimner.
1:45 PM — Tour the museum exhibits and the reconstructed Viking Quarter houses.
2:45 PM — Short hike up to the iconic Ansgar Cross, or a quick swim in Lake Mälaren.

Good to Know

Airport Transfer

Flygbussarna airport coach, around €12 — takes 40–45 minutes to the city centre.

Bergius Botanic Garden

Beautifully situated at Lake Brunnsviken. The Victoria House holds the world's largest water lily, with exotic plants in the Victoria House and Edvard Anderson Conservatory. Café, shop, and restaurant on site. Tuesday–Friday 11 AM – 4 PM, weekends 11 AM – 5 PM.

Free
Stockholm Archipelago Islands

The most popular islands include Fjäderholmarna, just a 30-minute ferry from downtown. Dalarö and Vaxholm are both about an hour away. SL is the public transport system and the local commuter ferry — just tap a credit card onboard, no app or pass needed. Strömkajen is the main central ferry terminal, right in front of the Grand Hôtel Stockholm.

Ferry Route 83 — Strömkajen to Vaxholm

A charming island town about an hour away, great for tourists and sightseeing with views of Stockholm's skyline along the way. Full of shops, restaurants, and historic sites — an old fortress across from the dock, once Sweden's "first line of defense," is now home to a Swedish history museum.

Ferry Route 89 — Klara Mälarstrand to Tappström

Heads west into Lake Mälaren, about an hour and ten minutes with pretty views along the way. Costs kr39 (~$3–4 USD). Starts at 7:40 AM weekday mornings, running every 40 minutes; every 2 hours on weekends.
